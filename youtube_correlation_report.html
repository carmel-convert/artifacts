YouTube × Plausible
Correlation Analysis

Jan 2025 – May 2026  ·  n = 17 months  ·  Spearman rank correlation

Abstract & Method

This analysis tests whether monthly YouTube activity metrics (watch hours, views, long-form and short-form video counts, impressions, CTR, likes, and subscriber delta) correlate with downstream outcomes tracked via Plausible: referral traffic and free trial clicks. The goal is to identify which YouTube inputs, if any, predict site-side conversions, and whether a 1-month lag improves those predictions.

Method: Spearman rank correlation was used throughout, chosen over Pearson because it is more robust to outliers and does not assume normally distributed data — appropriate for a small, real-world dataset with visible anomalies. All variables were tested same-month and with a 1-month lag (YouTube metric in month T predicting outcome in month T+1). Directional agreement — the percentage of consecutive months where both variables moved in the same direction — was also calculated as an N-independent cross-check.

Caveats: With n=17, even strong-looking correlations are unstable. Results should be treated as informed hypotheses for future experimentation, not conclusions. YouTube changed its Shorts view-counting methodology from April 2025 onwards (aligning with TikTok's system), creating a structural break in the Views series — Views correlations should be interpreted with extra caution.

What the signals mean

Impressions → referrals

r = +0.71 · same-month · strongest signal

When YouTube serves the content to more people, Plausible referrals go up the same month. This is the clearest proxy chain finding.

Note: impressions are an output of the algorithm, not a direct input.

Action What content types earn more impressions? Long-form count (r = +0.39 vs impressions) is the best candidate lever right now.

Subscriber delta → referrals (negative)

r = −0.54 same-month · −0.64 lagged · surprise finding

Months with higher subscriber growth correlate with fewer referrals, and this strengthens with a 1-month lag.

One likely explanation: subscriber growth months are driven by Shorts or broad content that attracts casual followers who never visit your site. High-referral months may be driven by niche long-form content that sends intent traffic, but doesn't grow the subscriber count.

Action Continue with niche long-form content that sends intent traffic, even if subscriber growth slows.

Long-form count → free trial clicks (lagged)

r = +0.40 · 1-month lag · improving signal

Publishing more long-form videos this month has a modest positive relationship with free trial clicks next month.

The lag makes intuitive sense. Someone watches a long video, considers the product, then converts. Not conclusive yet, but the directional logic is sound.

Action Track this signal over the next 6 months.

CTR vs impressions (negative)

r = −0.47 · reach/relevance tradeoff

High-impression months come with lower CTR.

This suggests impressions are driven by broad algorithmic distribution, not your existing audience finding content compelling.

Note: months where the algorithm is active may also be months with other traffic drivers operating simultaneously.

Watch hours, Views, Shorts — no signal yet

All near-zero correlations with both outcomes

Watch hours are trending upward (the best recent months: 76 and 87 hrs in Mar–Apr 2026) while referrals stay steady, suggesting content quality improvements haven't yet translated downstream (or the sample is simply too small to detect a relationship).

Views are additionally compromised by the April 2025 counting change. Short-form video count shows no positive relationship with any downstream metric.

Revenue proxy chain assessment

Publishing more long-form videos → earns more impressions → drives more referrals → leads to free trial clicks

Does the data support the proxy chain? Partially, and with important caveats.
  • The chain that holds: Impressions → Referrals (r = +0.71). This is real enough to act on as a hypothesis.
  • The chain that breaks: Publishing volume → Impressions. Long-form count has the best relationship (r = +0.39) but it's not strong enough to be confident. April 2026 had zero videos published and still generated 23,444 impressions.
  • The surprise: Subscriber growth moves opposite to referrals. Sub-growth and site conversion may be serving different audiences entirely.
  • The limit: n=17 means even r=0.71 could shift with a few more months of data. No p-values below 0.05 should be treated as statistically definitive at this sample size. The analysis will be meaningfully more reliable around n=24 (early 2027).
  • Recommended action: Track impressions and long-form count as your primary leading indicators. In 6 months, re-run this analysis — the long-form → impressions and long-form → free trial clicks signals are the ones most likely to either confirm or dissolve.
