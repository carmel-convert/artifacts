YouTube × Plausible
Correlation Analysis

Jan 2025 – Sep 2026  ·  n = 21 months (v1: n = 17)  ·  Spearman rank correlation

Abstract & Method

This analysis tests whether monthly YouTube activity metrics (watch hours, views, long-form and short-form video counts, impressions, YouTube impression CTR, likes and subscriber change) correlate with downstream outcomes tracked in Plausible: YouTube referral visitors, website exploration (bounce rate, visit duration, views per visit) and free trial clicks. The goal is to see which YouTube inputs, if any, predict site-side outcomes, and whether a 1-month lag improves those predictions.

Data: YouTube Studio monthly metrics plus Plausible metrics filtered to "YouTube is source", Jan 2025 – Sep 2026 (n = 21 months; v1 used Jan 2025 – May 2026, n = 17).

Method: Spearman rank correlation throughout, chosen over Pearson because it is more robust to outliers and doesn't assume normally distributed data, which suits a small, real-world dataset. Every variable is tested in the same month and with a 1-month lag (YouTube metric in month T vs outcome in month T+1). "Moves with referrals" (the share of months where both changed in the same direction) is a cross-check that doesn't depend on sample size. A year-on-year comparison uses Jan–Sep of each year.

Caveats: With n = 21, even strong-looking correlations can shift with a few more months of data. Results are informed hypotheses, not conclusions. YouTube changed how it counts views in April 2025 and again in August 2026, so Views correlations need extra caution.

Data notes: read before the numbers

  1. Views now has two structural breaks: April 2025 (Shorts counting change) and August 2026 (another view-counting change). "Engaged Views" only exists from August 2026 (2 months), so it isn't tested.
  2. June 2026: product-related videos introduced. Four months isn't enough to test this. Jun–Sep 2026 averaged 76 referrals/month vs 90 over the previous 12 months.
  3. September 2026: 3 MCP vertical ads rolled out, and ads traffic was filtered out of all YouTube Studio metrics.
  4. YouTube free trial clicks: an average of 0.25/month for Jun–Sep 2026, vs an average of 5.3/month over the previous 12 months. Plausible only credits YouTube when the visitor clicks a Free Trial button in the same visit, and descriptions link to informational resources.

What the signals mean

Impressions → referrals still holds

r = +0.58 · p = 0.006 · v1 +0.71

Still the clearest finding in the YouTube chain. It weakened. July 2026 is the main outlier: the lowest impressions since Apr 2025 (16.6k) but the second-highest referrals on record (145).

Impressions are still an algorithm output, not a direct input.

Action Keep impressions as the primary leading indicator. Long-form count vs impressions holds (+0.41) as the best candidate lever.

Subscriber delta → referrals (negative): holds

r = −0.50 same month · −0.43 lagged

Months with more subscriber growth still bring fewer referrals. The lagged version weakened (−0.64 → −0.43) and is no longer significant.

v1's explanation still fits: broad or Shorts-driven growth attracts casual followers who don't visit the site.

Action Keep optimising for intent traffic over subscriber count.

Long-form → free trial clicks: dissolved, for now

T+1 r = +0.15 · v1 +0.40

v1 marked this as the signal most likely to "confirm or dissolve". At n = 21 it has dissolved. Long-form output stayed steady (7.5/month) while FTCs fell to about 0.

If the FTC goal broke, this result isn't valid evidence either way.

Action Stop citing long-form → FTC as a signal. Re-test in Q4 after the site-wide FTC check.

YouTube impression CTR vs impressions is now significant

r = −0.50 · p = 0.021 · v1 −0.47 (p = 0.056)

YouTube impression CTR is the share of people who see a thumbnail on YouTube and click to watch. When impressions go up, this CTR goes down: YouTube is showing the videos to more people outside the core audience, and a smaller share of them click.

Jul–Sep 2026 is the same pattern from the other side: impressions were low (16.6k–22.2k/month) and YouTube impression CTR was high (2.4–2.9%, among the highest on record). Fewer, better-matched viewers.

Action Don't treat a lower YouTube impression CTR in high-impression months as a problem. Those months still send more visitors to the site (impressions → referrals r = +0.58).

New: YouTube visitors explore, and bounce less as volume grows

Bounce r = −0.56 · p = 0.009 · lag −0.50 · 2026 bounce 12% avg

Months with more YouTube visitors have a lower YouTube bounce rate (r = −0.56). So step 3 of the chain (referrals → website exploration) holds for YouTube on the bounce measure.

Year on year (Jan–Sep), visitors grew 31% and bounce fell from 26% to 12%: more people are clicking past the first page. But median visit duration fell from 40s to 14s, and visit duration has no link to visitor volume. More visitors explore beyond one page, but they don't stay longer.

Impressions go with lower bounce but shallower visits. Broad reach brings people who click once more, then leave.

Recommended proxies YouTube visitors (volume) + YouTube bounce rate (quality). Watch visit duration and check which pages YouTube links land on.

Watch hours, Views, Shorts, Likes: still no positive signal

All |r| ≤ 0.23 with referrals except Likes (−0.41, not significant)

Watch hours held around 44–54/month from Jun to Sep 2026 without moving referrals. Views now has two counting breaks. Short-form count has drifted slightly negative against both referrals (−0.23) and FTCs (−0.31). Shorts output went up (19.5/month vs 12 previously) while referrals didn't.

Revenue proxy chain assessment

Publishing more long-form videos → earns more impressions → drives more referrals → leads to free trial clicks

Does the data support the proxy chain? The middle of the chain holds, and both ends are still weak.
  • Holds: Impressions → Referrals (r = +0.58, p = 0.006). Weaker than v1 but still significant.
  • Still borderline: Long-form count → Impressions (+0.41, p = 0.066), essentially unchanged.
  • Dissolved: Long-form → FTC (lagged), from +0.40 to +0.15. It may be contaminated by the FTC drop.
  • Holds: Subscriber growth moves opposite to referrals (−0.50).
  • New, step 3 holds for YouTube: more YouTube visitors → lower bounce (−0.56, p = 0.009, YouTube-only data). Duration and views per visit: no link.
  • Step 4: YouTube visitors vs free trial clicks +0.34, not significant.
  • Q4 re-run (n = 24, Jan 2027): Run the site-wide FTC check first. If a direct trial CTA is added to descriptions, note the start month as a break. Do a first read of product-related videos with 7 months of data. Engaged Views will have 5 months, still too few to test.
